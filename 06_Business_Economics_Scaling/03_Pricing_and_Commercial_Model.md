# Pricing and Commercial Model

## Recommended Entry Model

The recommended first commercial offer is a **fixed-fee controlled paid pilot** for one automotive component programme.

The pilot should include:

- One approved risk-register template
- One programme and review team
- Limited configuration
- Synthetic, de-identified or customer-approved data
- Human confirmation of every AI-assisted finding
- Defined success measures and final evaluation report

## Pricing Principles

1. Price against customer value, not only model cost.
2. Keep the pilot scope narrow and contractually clear.
3. Separate implementation and configuration from recurring product access.
4. Avoid outcome guarantees that the product cannot control.
5. Do not publish a final price before buyer and willingness-to-pay research.

## Recurring Pricing Options

### Option A: Per Active Programme

Best fit when value is generated through repeated programme reviews. Simple for the buyer to understand.

### Option B: Platform Fee Plus Usage Tier

A base annual fee plus a tier based on monthly registers or risk-entry volume. Better alignment with processing costs but can reduce budget predictability.

### Option C: Reviewer-Seat Subscription

Easy to administer but may discourage wider adoption and does not directly reflect programme value.

## Recommended Future Structure

```text
Annual platform subscription
+ active-programme tier
+ one-time onboarding/configuration
+ optional integration and support services
```

## Pricing Evidence Required

- Economic buyer and budget owner
- Existing cost of manual review
- Number of active programmes
- Review frequency
- Procurement preference for subscription or services
- Acceptable pilot fee range
- Customer willingness to pay for integration and support

## Commercial Boundaries

RiskReview AI should not use gain-sharing based on avoided programme loss during the first pilot because attribution would be weak. Final pricing remains TBD until customer discovery and cost-to-serve analysis are completed.
