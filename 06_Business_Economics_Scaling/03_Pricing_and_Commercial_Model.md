# Pricing and Commercial Model

## 1. Commercial Position

RiskReview AI is positioned as an enterprise governance and assurance product, not as an autonomous certification or launch-approval service. Pricing should reflect workflow value, risk-criticality, integration effort, assurance requirements, number of programmes or sites, and support level.

## 2. Proposed Commercial Stages

### Discovery and Readiness Assessment

- Fixed scope of 4–6 weeks
- Data, workflow, governance, security, and value-baseline assessment
- Indicative planning range: **₹15–₹30 lakh**

### Controlled Pilot

- 12–16 weeks
- One domain and one programme, fleet, plant, or asset group
- Risk-register configuration, model-assisted review, Human-in-the-Loop workflow, shadow mode, and evaluation report
- Indicative planning range: **₹40–₹80 lakh**

### Annual Enterprise Subscription

- Annual software, monitoring, governance, and standard support
- Indicative planning range: **₹1.0–₹2.5 crore per year** for an initial enterprise deployment
- Integration, custom connectors, private deployment, and independent assurance priced separately

All price ranges are hypotheses requiring buyer interviews, competitive review, and paid-pilot evidence.

## 3. Pricing Drivers

- Number of programmes, sites, fleets, or asset groups
- Number and type of authorised users
- Monthly risk-record and evidence volume
- Model inference and monitoring intensity
- Required automotive or wind-energy configuration
- Private cloud, on-premises, or data-residency requirements
- Number of integrations and custom connectors
- Support response time and assurance frequency
- Supplier evidence-management scope

## 4. Recommended Pricing Structure

Use a hybrid structure:

1. **One-time implementation fee** for discovery, configuration, integration, data migration, and security approval.
2. **Annual platform subscription** for software, standard monitoring, audit features, and support.
3. **Usage or scale component** for additional programmes, sites, fleets, risk volume, or advanced model processing.
4. **Professional services** for custom assurance, domain validation, supplier onboarding, and independent review.

## 5. Value-Based Guardrail

Price should remain a reasonable share of verified customer value. A working commercial guardrail is **5%–15% of independently validated annual benefit**, subject to procurement norms, alternatives, implementation effort, switching cost, and risk allocation.

Scenario loss avoidance cannot be used alone to justify price. Verified workflow savings, avoided delay, audit efficiency, and customer willingness to pay should anchor the initial commercial offer.

## 6. Contract Terms

Commercial agreements should define:

- Intended and prohibited uses
- Human decision responsibilities
- Data ownership and processing rights
- Confidentiality and security controls
- Model, data, and supplier change notification
- Service levels and support boundaries
- Audit rights and evidence retention
- Incident notification and response
- Liability limitations and customer responsibilities
- Exit, deletion, portability, and transition support

## 7. Commercial Model Risks

- Long procurement and security-review cycles
- High customer-specific integration effort
- Limited access to representative data
- Unclear ownership between product, engineering, risk, and information technology budgets
- Supplier resistance to evidence sharing
- Overstated loss-avoidance assumptions
- Customer expectation of certification or guaranteed safety
- Lower willingness to pay before workflow value is proven

## 8. Pricing Validation Questions

- Who owns the problem and budget?
- What is the current annual cost of review, rework, delay, and audit preparation?
- Which outcome is sufficiently valuable to fund a pilot?
- Does the buyer prefer programme, site, fleet, user, or enterprise pricing?
- What implementation and security work is expected from the supplier?
- Which terms would prevent procurement or renewal?
- What evidence is required to move from pilot to annual contract?
