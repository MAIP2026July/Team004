## Slide 2: Enterprise Problem

AI-enabled products and operations create interconnected risks involving:

- Model and data drift
- Dataset bias and inadequate operating-condition coverage
- Connected-system cybersecurity and Over-the-Air update integrity
- Supplier black-box models
- Privacy and data sovereignty
- Explainability and human accountability
- Hardware and platform dependency
- Operational continuity after deployment

Traditional technical and project-risk reviews may not provide one traceable enterprise view joining technical evidence, business impact, human approval, and post-deployment monitoring.

## Slide 4: Proposed Framework

The framework combines:

1. Enterprise AI risk register
2. Deterministic validation
3. Model-assisted analysis
4. Responsible AI
5. Explainable AI
6. Human approval and escalation
7. Continuous monitoring and auditability

Framework references include NIST AI RMF 1.0, Responsible AI, Explainable AI, and Porter's Five Forces.

## Slide 5: Human–AI Governance

### AI Model Role

- Validate required information
- Analyse cause-event-impact structure
- Review evidence quality and freshness
- Identify potentially related risks
- Detect model, data, supplier, cybersecurity, and operational indicators
- Present rationale, uncertainty, limitations, and next action

### Human Role

- Approve likelihood and impact
- Accept, modify, reject, defer, or escalate findings
- Approve controls and exceptions
- Accept residual risk
- Make final Go/No-Go, launch, commissioning, restriction, rollback, and closure decisions

- Regional performance disparity
- Wind-turbine sensor drift
- Wake-steering outside validated conditions

- Unsupported ROI or loss-avoidance projection

Use the 5 × 5 likelihood-impact heatmap:

- 17–25: Critical
- 10–16: High
- 5–9: Medium
- 1–4: Low

## Slide 7: Responsible and Explainable AI

- Accountability and named decision owners
- Transparency between rules, model output, and human decisions
- Fairness and segmented evaluation
- Privacy and data minimisation
- Reliability, fallback, and rollback
- Security and update integrity
- Contestability and escalation
- SHAP, TCAV, LIME, Grad-CAM, Model Cards, and Data Sheets where technically suitable

Explainability output supports review but does not prove correctness or causality.

## Slide 8: Seven-Stage Rollout

1. Strategy and intended use
2. Risk and data readiness
3. Proof of Concept
4. Functional Prototype
5. Minimum Viable Product and validation
6. Shadow-mode pilot
7. Controlled deployment and scale

Each stage has a human approval gate and evidence requirements.

## Slide 9: Data, Model, and Evaluation

- Synthetic data for early pipeline testing
- Human labelling and disagreement adjudication
- Separate development, validation, and test data
- Deterministic rules for explicit checks
- AI model for contextual analysis
- Human review for material decisions

Proposed evaluation measures:

- Precision, recall, and F1
- Critical/High recall
- False positives and false negatives
- Human acceptance, rejection, modification, and escalation
- Explanation usefulness
- Review-time and rework change

## Slide 13: Launch Blockers and Decision Gates

Block or restrict progression when:

- Critical risk remains unresolved
- Mandatory control or human approval is missing
- Representative validation data is unavailable
- Cybersecurity or OTA integrity issue is unresolved
- Supplier evidence is inadequate
- Privacy or access issue remains open
- Fallback, rollback, or incident response is not tested
- Financial projections are presented as achieved outcomes without evidence
