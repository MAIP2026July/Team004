# Presentation Questions and Answers

## 1. What is the main problem being solved?

The framework addresses fragmented AI risk evidence across engineering, data, suppliers, cybersecurity, operations, finance, and governance. It creates one traceable process linking technical findings with enterprise impacts and accountable human decisions.

## 2. Why is AI required when rules can validate risk records?

Rules are appropriate for required fields, dates, ranges, formulas, and explicit approval conditions. AI is used for contextual tasks such as reviewing cause-event-impact wording, analysing evidence relevance, identifying related risks, detecting patterns or drift, and generating challenge questions.

## 3. Is RiskReview AI making autonomous decisions?

No. The model provides evidence-linked findings and recommendations. Authorised humans approve scores, controls, exceptions, residual risk, launch, commissioning, rollback, and closure.

## 4. Why are automotive and wind energy included together?

Automotive AI and ECU systems are the primary domain. Wind-turbine operations are a secondary domain used to test whether the governance core can transfer to another asset-intensive environment. Domain-specific data, controls, thresholds, reviewers, and evaluation remain separate.

## 5. What is the role of NIST AI RMF?

NIST AI RMF provides Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage functions for organising AI risk activities. The framework combines this with Responsible AI, Explainable AI, supplier strategy, human decisions, and product rollout gates.

## 6. What do SHAP and TCAV provide?

SHAP can estimate feature contributions for many models. TCAV can test model sensitivity to human-defined concepts where technically suitable. Neither method proves causality or model correctness. Domain experts must review the explanation together with validation evidence and known limitations.

## 7. How is fairness addressed?

Performance should be reviewed across relevant regions, operating conditions, asset types, and affected groups. The framework requires segmented evaluation, dataset-coverage review, disparity investigation, human oversight, and release restrictions when material gaps remain.

## 8. How is supplier black-box risk handled?

The framework requires Glass-Box evidence such as model and dataset documentation, validation results, cybersecurity controls, change notification, incident notification, audit rights, portability, and exit support. Missing critical supplier evidence may block launch or commissioning.

## 9. What is the ₹10 crore investment based on?

The ₹10 crore value is a proposed planning scenario allocated across six modules: data foundation, AI and rules, Responsible and Explainable AI, Human–AI workflow, monitoring and cybersecurity, and integration and pilot support. It requires supplier quotes and delivery estimates before funding approval.

## 10. Has the framework achieved 67% risk reduction?

No. The reduction from 17.2 Critical to approximately 5.6 Medium is based on the illustrative risk model and assumed control effectiveness. It is an expected scenario that must be validated through implemented controls, human-approved residual scores, and pilot evidence.

## 11. Is ₹710 crore annual loss avoidance verified?

No. It is a scenario-based projection. Verification requires incident probabilities, financial consequence evidence, attribution to the controls, implementation costs, and finance review.

## 12. Is the 7,000% ROI and one-week payback realistic?

These figures are mathematically derived from the current loss-avoidance scenario and ₹10 crore investment. They are not achieved results. Sensitivity analysis and measured pilot evidence are required before using them for an investment decision.

## 13. How will model quality be evaluated?

The evaluation uses human-labelled cases, separate development, validation, and test data, precision, recall, F1, Critical/High recall, false positives, false negatives, explanation usefulness, human modification, and workflow-time measures.

## 14. What prevents automation bias?

The interface separates model output from human-approved decisions, shows evidence and limitations, avoids preselecting acceptance, provides reject and escalate options, requires justification for material decisions, and monitors acceptance and override patterns.

## 15. What happens when the model is wrong?

The human reviewer rejects or modifies the finding. Critical errors are analysed individually. The system preserves the original output and decision, updates tests and controls, and may restrict use until revalidation.

## 16. What is the initial commercial model?

The recommended path is discovery, readiness assessment, functional MVP, paid shadow-mode pilot, controlled annual subscription, and later expansion. Pricing remains a hypothesis until validated with buyers and paid-pilot evidence.

## 17. What is the biggest implementation risk?

The largest risks are limited access to representative data, supplier opacity, integration effort, small labelled datasets, weak human-review capacity, and presenting scenario projections as achieved outcomes.

## 18. What is the next practical step?

Select one automotive AI or ECU programme, measure the baseline workflow, prepare approved data and labelled cases, run a controlled shadow-mode pilot, analyse model and human decisions, and update the business case with measured evidence.
