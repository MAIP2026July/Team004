# MVP-to-Revenue Roadmap

## 1. Purpose

This roadmap explains how RiskReview AI will move from a validated product concept to a controlled Minimum Viable Product (MVP), a paid pilot, and an initial recurring-revenue product.

The roadmap is designed for the selected starting market:

- **Industry:** Automotive
- **Customer Segment:** Tier 1 and Tier 2 automotive component manufacturers
- **Programme Type:** New Product Development and New Product Introduction
- **Lifecycle:** Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP) to Start of Production (SOP)
- **Primary Users:** NPD Project Manager and APQP Coordinator
- **Initial Workflow:** Monthly programme-risk and launch-readiness review

The commercial objective is not to sell a broad AI platform immediately. The objective is to prove that one automotive programme team will pay for a controlled product that improves risk-register quality and reduces review-preparation effort without automating consequential programme decisions.

## 2. Commercial Hypothesis

We believe an automotive component manufacturer may pay for RiskReview AI when the product can demonstrate all of the following:

1. Reduced preparation effort before monthly programme-risk reviews
2. Better completeness of risk owners, actions, dates, evidence and milestones
3. Earlier identification of unclear, duplicated or weakly controlled risks
4. Improved meeting readiness without replacing human programme judgement
5. Acceptable processing cost, reviewer effort and confidentiality controls

This hypothesis must be validated through stakeholder interviews, prototype testing and a controlled pilot. No willingness-to-pay claim is treated as proven at the current stage.

## 4. Stage 1: Discovery and Problem Validation

**Indicative period:** Weeks 1–2

### Objective

Confirm that monthly programme-risk review is a repeated and valuable problem for the selected automotive segment.

### Activities

- Interview at least five relevant stakeholders
- Include NPD Project Managers, APQP Coordinators, Programme Managers, Quality Managers or Manufacturing Engineering representatives
- Map the current risk-review preparation workflow
- Measure current preparation time and rework
- Identify common register-quality problems
- Confirm the economic buyer and approval process
- Understand confidentiality, adoption and integration concerns
- Test interest in a controlled pilot

### Evidence required

- At least three interviewed stakeholders confirm that risk-register preparation or quality is a recurring problem
- At least two stakeholders agree that the proposed workflow deserves prototype or pilot evaluation
- Current manual preparation time can be estimated
- The initial buyer, user and reviewer roles are clear
- No unresolved legal, confidentiality or workflow issue makes the concept unusable

### Decision gate

- **Proceed:** Repeated problem and credible pilot interest are confirmed
- **Rework:** Problem exists but customer, segment or workflow requires narrowing
- **Stop:** The problem is infrequent, low value or already solved adequately by existing tools

## 5. Stage 2: Clickable Prototype

**Indicative period:** Weeks 3–4

### Objective

Test whether target users understand and trust the proposed RiskReview AI workflow.

### Prototype scope

- One fictional automotive component programme
- One standard programme risk-register format
- Synthetic APQP-to-SOP risk entries
- Mandatory-field and overdue-action checks
- Cause-event-impact quality review
- Potentially related-risk comparison
- Mitigation challenge questions
- Evidence-freshness indicators
- Human actions: accept, edit, reject, link, merge or defer
- Verified review-agenda generation
- Audit-history preview

### Minimum evidence

- Users can complete the core workflow without extensive explanation
- Users understand the difference between a rule, an AI inference and a human decision
- Users can correct or reject findings
- No screen implies that AI has final authority over programme-risk decisions
- Main ambiguities and workflow changes are documented

### Decision gate

- **Proceed:** Workflow is understandable and users see practical value
- **Rework:** Core workflow is useful but roles, explanations or controls are unclear
- **Stop:** Users do not trust the workflow or the product adds unnecessary work

## 6. Stage 3: Controlled MVP

**Indicative period:** Months 2–3

### Objective

Evaluate whether the product improves one complete monthly programme-risk review under controlled conditions.

### MVP boundary

- One automotive component programme
- One approved risk-register template
- One Project Manager or APQP review team
- Approved non-sensitive, de-identified or synthetic data
- Human confirmation for every AI-assisted finding
- No automatic probability, impact, escalation, acceptance or closure
- No PPAP or Start-of-Production approval decision

### Evaluation measures

- Manual preparation time versus assisted preparation time
- Percentage of useful AI-assisted findings
- Acceptance, edit, rejection and defer rates
- Duplicate-suggestion precision on labelled examples
- Unsupported or fabricated suggestion rate
- Meaning-change incidents
- Human-review workload
- Processing cost per register
- User trust and willingness to continue

### Provisional success thresholds

These thresholds are hypotheses until validated:

- At least **25% reduction** in preparation time
- At least **70% useful-finding rate**, including accepted or usefully edited findings
- At least **80% precision** for related-risk suggestions on the held-out synthetic evaluation set
- Less than **5% unsupported-suggestion rate**
- **Zero automatic consequential programme decisions**
- **Zero accepted meaning-changing rewrite incidents**

### Decision gate

- **Proceed to paid pilot:** User value, safety and cost are acceptable
- **Proceed with conditions:** Value exists but defined controls or workflow changes are required
- **Rework:** Quality, workload or trust is below the required level
- **Stop:** Severe confidentiality, hallucination or meaning-change risk remains unresolved

## 7. Stage 4: First Paid Pilot

**Indicative period:** Months 3–6

### Commercial offer

A fixed-scope paid pilot for one automotive component programme.

### Proposed pilot package

- One programme and one approved risk-register template
- Initial configuration of customer terminology and milestones
- User onboarding for the Project Manager and APQP Coordinator
- Monthly programme-risk review support for an agreed pilot period
- Human approval for every finding
- Usage and quality monitoring
- End-of-pilot value and risk evaluation report
- Defined support and issue-escalation process

### Pricing approach

The initial paid pilot should use a **fixed pilot fee** because the scope, period and deliverables are controlled.

The pilot price should cover:

- Configuration effort
- Hosting or model usage
- Human support
- Evaluation and reporting
- Security and governance work
- A reasonable contribution margin

### Paid-pilot success criteria

- The customer completes the agreed monthly workflow
- Users continue using the product after onboarding
- Benefits are measurable against the manual baseline
- Human-review effort remains manageable
- No launch blocker is breached
- The buyer expresses interest in a recurring contract

## 8. Stage 5: First Recurring-Revenue Product

**Indicative period:** Months 6–12

### Commercial model

The recommended initial model is a B2B annual subscription with limited implementation support.

Possible pricing units include:

- Number of active automotive programmes
- Number of named reviewer seats
- Monthly risk-register volume
- Base platform fee plus configuration and integration charges

The final pricing unit should be selected only after observing actual usage and buyer preference.

### Initial subscription package

- RiskReview AI platform access
- Standard automotive programme-risk schema
- Configurable required fields and milestones
- Human-supervised AI review
- Audit history
- Defined support level
- Usage and quality reporting
- Model, prompt and rule version control

### Additional chargeable services

- Customer-specific taxonomy configuration
- Integration with approved programme-management systems
- Data migration
- Security and compliance review
- Training and onboarding
- Additional programme templates
- Premium support

## 9. Stage 6: Segment Expansion

Expansion should occur only after the first segment demonstrates repeatable value.

### Recommended sequence

1. Additional programmes within the first automotive component customer
2. Additional component categories within Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers
3. Supplier-quality and manufacturing-launch workflows
4. Other automotive programme stages
5. Adjacent project-based industries only after separate validation

Cross-industry expansion is not part of the initial MVP.

## 10. Build, Buy and API Evolution

### Prototype and early MVP

- Use deterministic rules for objective completeness checks
- Use an approved external or local language model for semantic review
- Use configurable prompts and a controlled risk-review rubric
- Store only fictional, synthetic or approved de-identified data

### Paid pilot

- Use enterprise-approved hosting and model services
- Apply role-based access control
- Maintain prompt, rule, model and dataset versions
- Add audit logging and manual fallback
- Measure cost per useful reviewed risk

### Recurring product

- Maintain provider replaceability
- Route simple checks to deterministic logic or smaller models
- Use larger models only for complex semantic review
- Add customer configuration without creating separate codebases for every customer

## 11. Cost-to-Serve Logic

The team will estimate:

```text
Cost per monthly programme review
=
Model or API processing cost
+ Hosting and storage
+ Human support and verification
+ Configuration effort
+ Monitoring and governance
+ Integration and maintenance allocation
```

The commercial offer must preserve an acceptable contribution margin after these costs.

## 12. Value Logic

The value case should be based on evidence such as:

```text
Value per review
=
Preparation hours saved
+ Rework avoided
+ Improved meeting readiness
+ Earlier identification of weak controls
- Product and human-review cost
```

RiskReview AI should not claim that it directly prevents project losses unless a controlled evaluation supports that claim.

## 13. Risks to Commercialisation

- Buyer is not clearly identified
- Users value the workflow but the organisation will not pay
- Human-review cost is too high
- Customer-specific configuration prevents scalable delivery
- Confidentiality blocks data use
- Users treat AI output as final judgement
- Model-provider cost or quality changes
- Existing APQP or programme tools add similar capability

Each risk will be reviewed before moving to the next commercial stage.

## 14. Evidence Status

At the current stage:

- The roadmap is a product and commercial hypothesis
- Discovery interviews are pending
- Willingness to pay is not yet validated
- Pilot pricing is not finalised
- Revenue and savings figures are not claimed
- Provisional MVP thresholds require controlled testing

## 15. Current Recommendation

Proceed with discovery and the clickable prototype. Do not commit to a production build or fixed commercial price until:

1. The repeated workflow problem is validated
2. The economic buyer is identified
3. The manual baseline is measured
4. The prototype demonstrates value and trust
5. Cost-to-serve is estimated
6. A customer expresses willingness to participate in a paid pilot
