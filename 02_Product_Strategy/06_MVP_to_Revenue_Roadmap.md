# MVP-to-Revenue Roadmap

## 1. Commercial Objective

Convert the academic RiskReview AI framework into a controlled enterprise governance product only after technical performance, governance effectiveness, user value, security, and commercial demand are validated.

## 2. Commercial Hypothesis

Automotive and wind-energy organisations may pay for a product that reduces AI risk-review effort, improves evidence traceability, strengthens supplier governance, and supports auditable launch or commissioning decisions.

This hypothesis must be tested with users, buyers, budget owners, procurement, information security, and legal stakeholders.

## 3. Roadmap

| Phase | Indicative period | Objective | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem validation | Months 0–2 | Confirm workflow pain and buyers | Interviews, baseline, sponsor, pilot use case |
| 2. MVP build | Months 2–4 | Implement risk intake, AI review, human decisions, and audit | Tested prototype and labelled scenarios |
| 3. Shadow-mode pilot | Months 4–6 | Compare AI output with expert decisions | Accuracy, override, usability, and governance results |
| 4. Paid controlled pilot | Months 6–9 | Validate willingness to pay and implementation effort | Signed pilot, security approval, value report |
| 5. Domain productisation | Months 9–15 | Standardise automotive or wind-energy configuration | Repeatable deployment package and support model |
| 6. Enterprise scaling | Months 15–24 | Portfolio reporting, integrations, and supplier workflows | Multiple programmes or sites, renewal evidence |

Timelines are planning estimates and depend on data access, integration, security review, and pilot complexity.

## 4. Initial Commercial Offering

A controlled pilot may include:

- One approved business unit, programme, fleet, or asset group
- Defined risk taxonomy and workflow configuration
- Import of an agreed risk dataset
- AI-assisted evidence and related-risk review
- Human decision and audit-log workflow
- Pilot evaluation and final value report

Pricing should be developed only after discovery of implementation effort, user count, data volume, integration needs, support model, and buyer budget.

## 5. Buyer and User Roles

| Role | Likely interest |
|---|---|
| Executive Risk, Product, or Operations leader | Business sponsor and risk outcome |
| Engineering, Quality, Cybersecurity, or Operations teams | Daily users and subject-matter reviewers |
| Information Technology and Security | Architecture, access, hosting, and controls |
| Procurement and Legal | Contract, supplier, privacy, liability, and audit terms |
| Finance | Business case and budget approval |

## 6. Revenue Options to Test

- Fixed-fee discovery and pilot
- Annual software subscription by programme, site, fleet, or portfolio
- Implementation and integration services
- Governance configuration and assurance support
- Supplier evidence-management module

No revenue model should be selected solely from internal assumptions. Buyer interviews and paid-pilot evidence are required.

## 7. Cost Drivers

- Data preparation and integration
- Cloud or on-premises infrastructure
- Model inference and monitoring
- Security and privacy controls
- Domain-expert review
- Customer support and implementation
- Independent assurance and audit
- Regulatory and contractual adaptation

## 8. Go-to-Market Sequence

1. Start with one high-value, evidence-rich workflow.
2. Secure an executive sponsor and operational owner.
3. Run in shadow mode before operational influence.
4. Produce an evidence-based value and risk report.
5. Convert to a paid controlled pilot.
6. Productise the strongest domain configuration.
7. Expand to adjacent programmes or sites.
8. Add cross-domain features only after repeatability is demonstrated.

## 9. Scale Gates

Scaling requires:

- Acceptable technical evaluation
- Complete human-decision and audit workflow
- Security and privacy approval
- Stable integration and support model
- Positive user adoption
- Measurable customer value
- Willingness to pay or renewal evidence
- Approved residual risk

## 10. Commercial Risks

- Long enterprise procurement cycles
- Limited access to sensitive data
- High integration cost
- Liability concerns
- Supplier resistance to transparency
- User distrust or over-reliance
- Different risk taxonomies across customers
- Insufficient differentiation from existing governance tools
