# Product Strategy

## 1. Strategic Direction

RiskReview AI has expanded from an automotive programme-risk review concept into an enterprise AI risk-governance framework. The product connects AI-supported risk identification and monitoring with business consequences, Responsible AI, Explainable AI, supplier governance, and accountable human decisions.

## 2. Primary and Secondary Domains

- **Primary domain:** Automotive AI, Electronic Control Units, Advanced Driver Assistance Systems, connected vehicles, Over-the-Air updates, fleet operations, and supplier AI.
- **Secondary domain:** Wind-turbine predictive maintenance, sensor monitoring, wake-steering optimisation, coordinated control, and operational continuity.

## 3. Strategic Problem

Enterprises lack a consistent mechanism to translate model, data, supplier, cybersecurity, and operational observations into explainable and auditable risk decisions. Fragmented evidence, supplier opacity, model drift, unclear accountability, and one-time validation can leave material exposure undiscovered or unresolved.

## 4. Product Vision

> Enable enterprises to identify, explain, govern, and continuously monitor AI-related risks while preserving accountable human authority over material decisions.

## 5. Strategic Pillars

1. Structured enterprise risk register
2. AI-assisted assessment and monitoring
3. Human-in-Command, Human-in-the-Loop, and Human-on-the-Loop governance
4. Responsible AI and Explainable AI controls
5. Supplier and third-party governance
6. Risk-based launch and commissioning gates
7. Continuous assurance and auditability

## 6. Differentiation

RiskReview AI does not position AI as the final decision-maker. The product combines deterministic checks, contextual AI analysis, domain review, governance thresholds, and traceable human approval. The common governance core can be configured for automotive and wind-energy use cases.

## 7. Strategic Outcomes

- Earlier discovery of model, data, supplier, or cybersecurity risks
- Better evidence traceability
- Consistent risk prioritisation
- Stronger supplier transparency
- Faster preparation for launch, commissioning, and risk reviews
- Continuous post-deployment monitoring

## 8. Scope Evolution



The initial use case focused on AI-assisted review of programme risk registers used in automotive New Product Development (NPD) and New Product Introduction (NPI), particularly from Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP) to Start of Production (SOP).

### Initial Users

- NPD Project Manager
- APQP Coordinator
- Risk Owner
- Quality Engineer
- Manufacturing Engineer
- Supplier Quality Engineer
- Programme Leadership

### Initial Workflow

1. Upload a programme risk register.
2. Validate mandatory fields and dates.
3. Review cause-event-impact wording.
4. identify vague mitigation actions and stale evidence.
5. Suggest potentially related risks.
6. Present observations for human review.
7. Record acceptance, rejection, or modification.
8. Prepare a verified management-review agenda.

### Initial Decision Boundary

AI could identify, analyse, and recommend. Authorised humans retained control over risk scoring, escalation, acceptance, closure, and launch decisions.

## 3. Reason for Expanding the Scope

The initial workflow addressed the quality of programme risk records, but enterprise adoption of AI creates wider risks across products, operations, data, cybersecurity, suppliers, and post-deployment monitoring.

The project was expanded to answer a broader question:

> How can organisations govern AI-related enterprise risks from initial strategy and model assessment through launch, deployment, continuous monitoring, and accountable human decision-making?

The expansion allows the project to demonstrate:

- Enterprise-level business relevance
- Cross-functional AI governance
- Technical and non-technical risk integration
- Responsible AI and Explainable AI controls
- Supplier and platform dependency analysis
- Risk-based launch and commissioning gates
- Transferability across industrial domains

## 4. Expanded Scope

### 4.1 Primary Domain: Automotive AI and ECU Systems

The primary domain covers:

- Electronic Control Units (ECUs)
- Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS)
- Automated-driving perception systems
- Connected-vehicle infrastructure
- Over-the-Air (OTA) updates
- Fleet telemetry
- Automotive AI suppliers
- AI hardware and software platforms

Representative risks include edge-case perception failure, thermal-latency drift, adversarial manipulation, OTA compromise, supplier opacity, privacy exposure, data bias, vendor lock-in, and model drift.

### 4.2 Secondary Domain: Wind-Turbine Operations

Wind-turbine operations are included to demonstrate transferability of the governance framework. Representative use cases include predictive maintenance, sensor analysis, wake-steering optimisation, coordinated control, and operational monitoring.

The secondary domain introduces risks involving model opacity, false maintenance recommendations, sensor drift, coordinated-control failure, cybersecurity, continuity, and supplier bottlenecks.

### 4.3 Enterprise Governance Layer

The enterprise layer covers:

- Risk identification and ownership
- Operational, financial, data, ethical, cybersecurity, and continuity impacts
- Inherent and residual risk scoring
- Human approval and escalation gates
- Responsible AI
- Explainable AI
- Third-party and supplier governance
- Product maturity
- Phased rollout
- Continuous monitoring and auditability

## 6. Current In-Scope Items

- Automotive AI and ECU risk scenarios
- Wind-turbine AI operational risk scenarios
- AI-assisted detection, assessment, and monitoring
- Human-in-Command, Human-in-the-Loop, and Human-on-the-Loop governance
- A 5 × 5 likelihood-impact methodology
- Responsible AI and Explainable AI controls
- Supplier and platform dependency
- Technical, governance, and business evaluation
- Seven-stage implementation roadmap

## 7. Current Out-of-Scope Items

- Safety or regulatory certification
- Autonomous approval of launch or commissioning
- Replacement of formal engineering validation
- Replacement of cybersecurity testing
- Production deployment across an actual enterprise
- Unsupervised acceptance or closure of material risks
- Claims of measured commercial value without pilot evidence

## 8. Scope-Control Rules

Any new use case must satisfy all of the following before being added:

1. It represents an identifiable enterprise decision or risk.
2. AI provides value beyond a simple fixed-rule check.
3. Required data and evidence can be described.
4. Accountable human roles are identified.
5. Failure consequences and escalation thresholds are defined.
6. Evaluation measures are available.
7. The addition does not imply unsupported certification or deployment.
