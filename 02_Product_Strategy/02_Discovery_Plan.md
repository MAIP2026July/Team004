# Discovery Plan

## 1. Discovery Objective

Validate whether automotive and wind-energy organisations experience significant difficulty connecting AI technical assurance with enterprise risk decisions, and determine whether RiskReview AI can create measurable operational and governance value.

## 2. Key Hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | AI risk evidence is fragmented across functions and systems | Interview examples and workflow maps |
| H2 | Launch or commissioning reviews require excessive manual preparation | Baseline preparation time and rework data |
| H3 | Supplier AI transparency is insufficient | Missing model, data, validation, update, or incident evidence |
| H4 | Users need explanations and uncertainty, not only risk scores | Usability tests and reviewer feedback |
| H5 | Human approval and override improve trust and accountability | Acceptance, rejection, modification, and escalation patterns |
| H6 | A common governance core can serve automotive and wind-energy contexts | Cross-domain workflow comparison |
| H7 | Buyers will fund a controlled pilot | Budget-owner interviews and willingness-to-pay evidence |

## 4. Research Methods

1. **Semi-structured interviews:** 12–18 participants across the three stakeholder groups.
2. **Workflow observation:** Observe at least two risk, launch, commissioning, or maintenance-review sessions where access permits.
3. **Artifact review:** Examine anonymised risk registers, validation summaries, supplier evidence lists, incident records, and approval templates.
4. **Concept testing:** Test the risk dashboard, explanation panel, human decision workflow, and escalation model.
5. **Prototype evaluation:** Use representative or synthetic scenarios with documented expected results.

## 5. Interview Questions

- Which AI-related decisions are hardest to prepare or approve?
- Where is supporting evidence stored?
- Which gaps most often delay review, launch, or commissioning?
- What supplier information is unavailable or unreliable?
- Which AI recommendations require explanation or uncertainty information?
- Who can accept, reject, override, or escalate the recommendation?
- What would make the output untrustworthy?
- What measurable outcome would justify a paid pilot?

## 6. Data to Capture

- Current workflow steps and duration
- Systems and documents used
- Decision owners and approval gates
- Frequent evidence gaps
- Risk categories and terminology
- False-alert and missed-alert consequences
- Security and confidentiality constraints
- Expected integrations
- Buyer, user, and budget owner

## 7. Discovery Success Criteria

Proceed to a controlled pilot when:

- At least three independent participants confirm the same material workflow problem.
- A measurable baseline exists for review effort, evidence quality, or unresolved actions.
- An authorised business owner agrees to sponsor the pilot.
- Representative data or synthetic test scenarios are available.
- Human decision rights and escalation paths are defined.
- Security, privacy, and supplier constraints are understood.

## 8. Ethics and Responsible Research

- Use anonymised or synthetic data unless formal permission exists.
- Do not collect unnecessary personal or proprietary information.
- Explain how interview and test data will be used.
- Record assumptions separately from observed evidence.
- Avoid presenting participant opinion as validated enterprise fact.

## 9. Discovery Outputs

- Interview summary
- Current-state workflow
- Problem-evidence matrix
- Prioritised user needs
- Updated Jobs to Be Done
- MVP recommendation
- Pilot success measures
- Risks, assumptions, issues, and dependencies log
- Willingness-to-pay evidence
